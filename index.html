The proposition

Three ways to book it

I am looking for a room in Nashville to host an intimate evening through October 2026. Ten to twelve nights across the month suits me best, one or two shows a night, but some rooms would rather start with a single evening. Both work.

How the money is handled is your call. There are three ways, and I have worked all three.

  1. You hire the show

    A flat fee for the night. You keep every ticket you sell and the bar, and there is nothing to negotiate afterward. This is how the Fauquier History Museum booked it, twice.

  2. We split the door

    No fee up front and no rent. We agree a share of the ticket money, so a night that sells badly costs you nothing. I handle the ticketing and the press campaign.

  3. You rent me the room

    I pay for the night, I sell the tickets and I carry the risk. Some rooms prefer it that way. I am open to it.

You keep the bar in all three. I take no part of it. People come to a seance for the atmosphere, so they arrive early and they stay afterward. That is two bar visits a head on a night your room would otherwise be quiet.

What I ask for in every version is your audience. An evening only fills when both of us point our people at it. I supply the artwork, the posters and the copy, sized and written and ready to go. Your part is your own channels.

Why this October

One hundred years since Houdini died

Houdini died on the thirty first of October, 1926. This October opens the centennial year, and I have built a version of the show about Houdini.

The centennial runs a full year, to the thirty first of October 2027. October is where I want to open. If October does not work for your room, the anniversary does not close when Halloween does.

This is the premium version of the evening. It is built for the centennial, it costs me more to stage, and it will carry a higher ticket than my usual parlour night.

What makes it more than atmosphere is that it is true. Houdini spent the last years of his life exposing fraudulent mediums, so a seance performed in his centennial year is not a novelty. It is the subject. Your audience will have grown up hearing his name. Very few of them will know what he was actually doing at the end.

It is a real news story, which is not something an October evening usually has, and I am already working the local press on it.

I spent my career duplicating Houdini's feats and more. But when I need historical information, I always rely upon Carnegie.

Steve "Mr. Escape" Baker

What the room gets

Terms to suit the room

Hire the show, split the door, or rent me the night. You pick the one that fits how you already work.

The bar, entirely

I take no part of it. An audience that arrives early and stays late is worth more than the door.

Your nights

Weekends are your most valuable slots and I do not expect them. Early shows and quieter nights work just as well.

I bring the campaign

My own list, a centennial press campaign, the artwork and the ticketing. You are not being asked to build it, only to put your name behind it.

What I ask of the room

Your channels

Socials, email list, website calendar. I supply everything finished, so it is a post rather than a project.

Posters up

I print them and deliver them.

Your listings

Whatever local calendars and press you already feed each month.

One named contact

Someone who runs the room on show nights.
